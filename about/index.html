The study

Is a memory champion built, or born?

A Stanford study, run at the Max Planck Institute of Psychiatry in Munich, Germany, put 51 people through the kind of recall test that competitive memorizers do for sport.

Everyone sat that test twice — once cold, and once again six weeks later. What happened in between was the experiment.

The test

Memorize seventy-two words

  1. One word at a time Each of the 72 words appears alone on a screen for 2 seconds, with a 2–5 second gap before the next.
  2. A break every six words 30 seconds of rest — and participants are told not to rehearse what they have just seen.
  3. Twenty minutes later 10 minutes to write down as many of the 72 words as they can, in any order.
  4. Twenty-four hours later A second 10-minute free-recall test, this time taken over the phone.

The split

Three groups. One difference.

The 51 were divided into three groups of 17 and sent away for six weeks. Two groups trained every day; the third was left alone.

Then all 51 came back and took the same test again.

The training

What the mnemonic group actually did

  1. Four routes in the first two weeks Familiar journeys, learned by heart — a fixed set of places to leave things.
  2. Thirty minutes a day, on memocamp.com Every day for six weeks.
  3. Five minutes in, fifteen minutes out Study the words for 5 minutes; spend 15 recalling and writing them back.
  4. A weekly visit to the lab An interview about the regime, a supervised session, and a plan for the week ahead.
You don't memorize a list. You walk somewhere you already know, and collect what you left there.

What to do next

Start tonight, in five moves

  1. Read everything about memory on memocamp.com It is the site the study's participants trained on. Start where they started. -> memocamp.com
  2. Build your first route — your own body, 20 items Feet to hair. You already know it perfectly, which is the whole point. -> Body Route
  3. Then a place you know Your flat, your walk to work, your childhood house. Fix the order and don't change it.
  4. Memorize by story, not by effort Give every item on the route something absurd to do with the thing you're storing there.
  5. Keep building routes Every new route is more slots. The ceiling is how many places you've learned.
